# AML Investigations Capacity & KPIs

A worked example of monthly reporting for an anti-money-laundering (AML) investigations team: KPI and KRI definitions in SQL, a six-month alert-volume forecast, and an investigator capacity model. Everything runs on seeded synthetic data.

> **All data in this repository is synthetic and illustrative.** It is generated by a seeded simulator. No real institution's figures, rates or volumes are used or claimed, and results should be read as a demonstration of the method.

## Operational problem

An AML alert queue has to keep alerts within service-level targets (SLAs) while volume trends upward, shifts seasonally and jumps when a detection rule is launched, and while investigator headcount changes through attrition and new-hire ramp-up. When arrivals exceed what the team can close, the backlog grows, alerts age past SLA, including High-risk alerts with a 5-day target. Operations leaders need risk indicators (KRIs) that flag SLA exposure early, a view of productivity (KPIs), a volume forecast and an estimate of how many investigators are required.

## Data

`src/simulate.py` generates about 100,000 alerts, 10,000 cases and a roster of about 40 investigators (25 at the start) over 30 months (2024-01 to 2026-06), with a fixed seed. Alert volume combines trend, seasonality, noise and one documented shock (a new detection rule launched June 2025). Each alert carries a creation time, rule type, risk band and score, SLA target, handle time, assigned investigator and disposition; escalated alerts open cases that end in a SAR filing or not. Every assumption and parameter is listed in [`docs/simulation_assumptions.md`](docs/simulation_assumptions.md).

## Approach

1. SQL KPI/KRI layer (`sql/`, DuckDB): staging views, calendar and roster dimensions, and marts for monthly KPIs and KRIs, aging snapshots, scenario-level rates, investigator productivity, a SAR-timeliness KRI and a RAG scorecard. A small runner (`src/models.py`) executes the files in order. Definitions, grain and thresholds are in [`docs/metric_definitions.md`](docs/metric_definitions.md).
2. Forecast (`src/forecast.py`): OLS regression on monthly alert volume with linear trend, one annual sine/cosine pair and a post-launch step. A six-month holdout backtest reports MAPE; a six-month forecast carries 95% prediction intervals.
3. Capacity model (`src/capacity.py`): required FTE = forecast volume x handle time / productive hours per FTE (utilization, attendance and business days), compared with ramp-weighted available FTE under three scenarios: base, +20% volume from a further rule launch, and an attrition shock.
4. Reporting (`src/figures.py`, `src/report.py`): four figures and a memo, with every number generated from the model outputs.

## Results

Illustrative results on the simulated data (seed 42; as of June 2026). They are copied from the memo, [`reports/kpi_kri_memo.md`](reports/kpi_kri_memo.md), which `make all` regenerates; rerun at the same seed to reproduce them.

| Metric | Value | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Open backlog (month end) | 2,811 | Red (threshold 2,500) |
| % closed within SLA | 39.5% | Red (target 95%) |
| Open alerts aged over 30 days | 39.0% | Red |
| Open alerts aged over 60 days | 0.0% | Green |
| High-risk open alerts past SLA | 88.6% | Red |

SLA breach rate (60.5%) and false-positive rate (90.8%) are the complements of % within SLA and escalation rate, so they are not listed separately.

The holdout backtest MAPE on the last six months is 5.1%, against 14.0% for a seasonal-naive forecast. The model estimates that the rule launch added about 476 alerts per month.

In the base case the staffing gap averages about -0.7 FTE and peaks at +2.0 FTE in November 2026, a 21-business-day month; the highest 3-month average gap is +0.6 FTE. With +20% volume the gap averages +3.9 FTE and peaks at +8.0. With the attrition shock it averages +2.4 and peaks at +6.1. Clearing the current backlog over six months adds about 3.1 FTE.

Investigators with 12 or more months of tenure close a median of 33.2 alerts per week (P10 to P90, 29.1 to 38.0); investigators in their first three months close a median of 20.7.

## How to run

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
make all     # simulate -> load -> SQL models -> forecast -> capacity -> figures -> report
make test    # pytest: simulator, SQL KPI correctness on a fixture, capacity formula
```

Generated data and intermediate outputs go to `data/` and `outputs/` (git-ignored); the memo and figures are written to `reports/`. Python 3.12 or later (the pinned numpy 2.5.2 requires it), with the versions in `requirements.txt`: numpy, pandas, DuckDB, statsmodels, matplotlib and pytest.

## Limitations

- The data is simulated; relationships between volume, handle time, staffing and backlog follow the stated assumptions and are not evidence about any real operation.
- The forecast is fitted on 30 monthly observations with a single seasonal harmonic. Prediction intervals reflect model error only, not future rule changes.
- The capacity model uses average handle time and one utilization figure, and ignores skill mix and holidays.
- Case (L2) work uses no investigator capacity in the simulator, and escalated and non-escalated alerts share handle-time distributions.
- The forecast has the same form as the process that generated the data, so its MAPE is flattering. The seasonal-naive comparison is a weak baseline because it ignores trend and the rule launch.
- Pending backfills for recent leavers are inferred from the roster at the midpoint of the backfill lag range, since actual start dates are not available.
- RAG thresholds are placeholders chosen for illustration, not policy or regulatory limits.
- Before the rule launch, simulated capacity exceeds demand, so the pre-launch backlog is near zero, which is cleaner than most real queues.
